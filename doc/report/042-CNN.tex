\subsection{Classification Performance}

The CNN classifier was evaluated under both a centralized baseline and
a federated training regime. In the centralized setting, the model
reached a test accuracy of 83.7\%, an AUC of 77\%, and a recall of
93\%. Under federated learning, the classifier achieved 81\% accuracy,
a higher AUC of 90\%, and a recall of 92\%.

These outcomes show that federated training can preserve strong
clinical sensitivity while improving the model's ability to
distinguish pneumonia from normal cases. The federated training
performance is likely better due to the identical distribution of data
(IID), which typically makes model training through federation
simpler. More experiments would be needed using different data splits
to conclude if FL can \textit{always} be equivalent to centralized
training in model convergence. The higher AUC for the
federated model indicates better overall ranking performance, even
when accuracy remains slightly lower than the centralized baseline.

High recall is particularly important for pneumonia screening, and
both approaches maintain this property. The federated model therefore
represents a viable alternative for distributed medical classification
systems, balancing privacy-preserving training with competitive
diagnostic performance.
